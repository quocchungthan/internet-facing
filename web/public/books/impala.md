# 1967 Chevrolet Impala — a field book

**Learning configuration:** United States Sport Sedan four-door hardtop,
283 cubic-inch small-block V8, Powerglide two-speed automatic, rear-wheel drive.
This is one selected factory-era combination, not a specification for every
1967 Impala or any particular surviving car.

**Content contract:** [impala.json](https://github.com/quocchungthan/internet-facing/blob/sub/30s/web/src/content/impala.json).
Each chapter anchor is the corresponding `parts[].id`. Interactive links
select a part at the site's root.

## Identify before maintaining

Start with the [GM Heritage 1967 Chevrolet information kit][kit] and
[archive][archive]. A model-year kit describes a range of cars. It does not
prove what is installed in a car today. The full-size range used a 119-inch
wheelbase; several engines, transmissions and equipment options existed.

Document identification numbers, engine casting information, installed
transmission, brake equipment and restoration records. Do not infer the engine
from a badge or photograph. Engine swaps, disc-brake conversions, aftermarket
wheels, ignition changes and electrical repairs are common in surviving classics.

The schematic's overall body dimensions are approximate silhouette parameters.
They must not be used for parts fitment, alignment or structural measurement.

## Before waking a stored classic

A car that ran when parked is not automatically safe now. Ask a qualified
classic-car specialist to assess fuel leakage, brake hydraulics, tire age,
cooling, wiring, structural corrosion and lubrication before attempting use.
Fresh paint and deep tire tread do not establish roadworthiness.

Work in a safe, ventilated environment with ignition sources controlled.
Never run an engine in an enclosed garage. Do not support a car with an
emergency jack alone, compress springs or open brake and fuel circuits based
on this book.

<a id="engine"></a>
## Small-block V8

[Explore the V8](/?car=impala&part=engine).
Contract: `impala.parts[engine]`.

The learning engine is a 283 cubic-inch Chevrolet small-block, arranged
longitudinally. Eight cylinders form two banks. A central camshaft operates
the overhead valves through lifters, pushrods and rocker arms. Connecting rods
turn the crankshaft, which delivers torque toward the rear.

Check actual engine identity before selecting parts. Rebuilds may change the
camshaft, compression, ignition, induction and service needs. A factory-era
rating is not a measured output for a rebuilt engine; no output figure is
asserted here.

Record smoke, sounds, pressure changes and operating conditions. Severe
knocking, pressure loss, overheating or fuel leakage means stop and seek help.

<a id="lubrication"></a>
## Oil & lubrication

[Explore the oil system](/?car=impala&part=lubrication).
Contract: `impala.parts[lubrication]`.

A wet sump stores oil. The pump, filter and galleries deliver it to bearings
and other loaded surfaces. The installed camshaft and rebuild specification
matter when choosing a lubricant.

Online arguments about flat-tappet additives are not an engine-specific
prescription. Confirm requirements with the engine builder and correct shop
manual; do not mix an improvised additive recipe.

Use the procedure appropriate to the installed dipstick and sump. Track oil
consumption and leaks over time. Never keep running an engine after loss of
oil pressure.

<a id="intake"></a>
## Carburetor & intake

[Explore the intake](/?car=impala&part=intake).
Contract: `impala.parts[intake]`.

In the selected carbureted configuration, air passes through an air cleaner
and carburetor, where mechanical passages meter fuel into the airflow. A
choke affects cold operation. The installed carburetor and linkage may differ
from the original equipment.

Identify the actual unit before applying adjustments. Record whether starting
problems occur cold or hot. Have air-cleaner condition, linkage and choke
operation checked professionally.

**Fire risk:** never pour fuel into the intake, peer into a carburetor while
cranking, or start an engine with fuel wetness or a strong fuel smell.

<a id="cooling"></a>
## Radiator & cooling

[Explore heat rejection](/?car=impala&part=cooling).
Contract: `impala.parts[cooling]`.

Coolant carries heat through the engine and radiator. The thermostat, pump,
fan, shroud and airflow all matter. Restorations may use replacement radiators,
different pulleys or electric fans; their suitability must be evaluated as a
system.

When cold, inspect accessible hoses and seams. A hose can degrade internally
despite looking acceptable outside. Repeated temperature problems need
diagnosis, not automatic substitution of a thermostat.

Never remove a hot pressure cap. Keep hands and clothing away from fans,
pulleys and belts. A retrofitted electric fan can start unexpectedly.

<a id="transmission"></a>
## Powerglide & driveline

[Explore the rear-drive path](/?car=impala&part=transmission).
Contract: `impala.parts[transmission]`.

Powerglide provides two forward ratios. A torque converter couples the engine
to the automatic transmission. A propeller shaft carries torque rearward to a
differential, which allows the driven wheels to turn at different speeds in
a corner.

Other transmissions were available in the model range. Identify the installed
unit and any rebuild details. Ask the rebuilder or a qualified specialist for
correct fluid requirements and checking procedure.

Report slipping, delayed engagement, clunks or vibration. Do not inspect a
rotating shaft underneath a running, raised vehicle.

<a id="brakes"></a>
## Braking system

[Explore the braking principle](/?car=impala&part=brakes).
Contract: `impala.parts[brakes]`.

Brake equipment and optional assist varied, and surviving cars may have disc
conversions. Identify the master cylinder, assist, lines, hoses and wheel
brakes together. A schematic disc is not evidence of original factory fitment.

Have a specialist check condition, compatibility, leaks and roadworthiness,
especially after storage. A soft or sinking pedal, pulling, grinding or fluid
leakage requires attention before driving.

Old friction material may contain asbestos. Do not blow dust with compressed
air or improvise a cleaning procedure. Arrange professional recovery if brakes
are impaired.

<a id="wheels"></a>
## Wheels & tires

[Explore wheels and tires](/?car=impala&part=wheels).
Contract: `impala.parts[wheels]`.

Tires age even if a car travels very little. Read date codes and inspect cracks,
bulges, wear and pressure loss. [NHTSA's tire guidance][tires] explains general
safety concepts, but it does not prescribe pressures for a modified classic.

Replacement sizing must account for wheel width, offset, load rating,
construction and brake clearance. Historical bias-ply sizing is not permission
to choose a guessed radial equivalent. Get specialist advice for the actual
vehicle and its wheels.

Check cold pressures and the spare. Deep tread does not make an aged tire safe.
Do not use the 3D model to infer jacking locations or fastener torque.

<a id="suspension"></a>
## Suspension & steering

[Explore suspension](/?car=impala&part=suspension).
Contract: `impala.parts[suspension]`.

The front wheels are independently located; the rear uses a live axle.
Springs support load while dampers control oscillation. Steering joints,
ball joints and bushings determine how predictably the wheels are guided.

Report wandering, clunks and uneven ride height. Have wear inspected before
requesting alignment: adjusting geometry cannot cure a worn joint. A car with
unknown or modified suspension needs assessment of the installed components.

Coil springs store dangerous energy. Do not loosen suspension fasteners or
attempt spring removal from an illustration.

<a id="electrical"></a>
## Ignition & electrics

[Explore ignition](/?car=impala&part=electrical).
Contract: `impala.parts[electrical]`.

A distributor-based ignition directs high voltage to spark plugs in sequence.
Correct timing and reliable wiring are important. Many classic cars use
electronic ignition conversions, so points-era adjustment instructions may
not apply.

Record the installed ignition, battery, charging hardware and wiring changes.
Have brittle insulation, poor grounds and slow cranking investigated. Do not
replace components solely because a similar car had the same symptom.

Ignition voltage can injure. Never pull leads from a running engine, bridge
battery terminals or disconnect the battery while running.

<a id="fuel"></a>
## Tank & fuel delivery

[Explore fuel delivery](/?car=impala&part=fuel).
Contract: `impala.parts[fuel]`.

Fuel moves from the rear tank toward the engine's pump and carburetor. Hose
age, compatibility, routing and tank contamination matter. Pump conversions
must not be assumed safe because the engine starts.

Before starting after storage, have stale fuel, leaks and tank condition
assessed. A hose that physically fits is not necessarily suitable for the
fuel in use.

If fuel leaks, do not start the engine. Avoid sparks and flames and seek
qualified help. Fuel-line opening and repair are outside this book.

<a id="exhaust"></a>
## Exhaust system

[Explore exhaust routing](/?car=impala&part=exhaust).
Contract: `impala.parts[exhaust]`.

Manifolds collect exhaust from both V8 banks. Pipes and silencers carry it
toward the rear. Single- and dual-exhaust systems exist; this illustration
does not decode factory options or certify a modified installation.

Have new ticking, rattling, leaks and clearances inspected. Exhaust smell in
the cabin needs urgent attention. Never run the engine in an enclosed garage:
carbon monoxide can be fatal, even when it is not detected by smell.

<a id="body"></a>
## Body, frame & access

[Explore the body](/?car=impala&part=body).
Contract: `impala.parts[body]`.

The four-door Sport Sedan silhouette represents body-on-frame construction.
Frame condition, body mounts, structural rust and previous repairs are
safety concerns, not just restoration details.

Have a knowledgeable specialist inspect structural areas. Check lighting,
wipers, mirrors, belts and latch engagement. Confirm the hood latches securely
before moving the car.

The animated panels show access as a concept. They do not reproduce actual
hinges, support arrangements, lift points or safe repair locations.

## Build a history, not a guess

Keep identity records, photographs of installed components, specialist
assessments, restoration receipts, dates and odometer readings. Record what
has been verified and what is still unknown.

Use the appropriate model-year shop manual, supplements and component
manufacturer or rebuilder documentation. This book intentionally supplies no
unverified fluid grades, capacities, tire pressures, ignition settings,
torque figures or universal service intervals.
